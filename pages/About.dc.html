Why DSAMaxxing exists

I built the thing I needed
in that exam hall.

DSAMaxxing is made by one developer in India, nights and weekends, for everyone who has ever stared at a coding question and felt their mind go blank.

M left = 0, right = n − 1
Mahikshith Solo developer · India
Every character, every hint and every “explain it like I'm a kid” button on this site is something I wish I'd had.

I'm Mahikshith, a software developer in India. In my final year of engineering I sat for campus placement test after placement test. I would get through the aptitude round. Then the coding round would begin, and my mind would go completely blank.

It wasn't that I hadn't practised. I had a spreadsheet of hundreds of “solved” problems. But I had memorised answers, not understood them. Change one small detail — sorted instead of unsorted, a pair instead of a triple — and I was lost all over again.

The night it finally clicked, I was on a late train home, drawing Two Sum on the back of my ticket: two people walking toward each other along a platform, one step at a time. Suddenly the code was obvious. It had never been about the code. It was about the story.

I got my job eventually, the long way round. But I kept thinking about everyone still sitting in that exam hall, feeling stupid about something that was never their fault. Nobody had told them the story yet.

So I started building DSAMaxxing, alone, after work: one story, one animation, one tiny step at a time. Lumi and Roo were the first two characters. Old Hoot came later, because I remembered how much I needed someone to nudge me without just handing me the answer.

If you're struggling with DSA right now, I want you to hear this from someone who was exactly there: you're not bad at this. You just haven't met the right story yet. Let's find it together.

— Mahikshith
